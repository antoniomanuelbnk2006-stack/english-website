## In one sentence

The **imperative** is the form we use to tell someone what to do: *Abre la puerta*, *No corras*, *Siéntese*.

## Form

| | affirmative | negative |
|---|---|---|
| **tú** | present *él* form: *habla, come, vive* | *no hables, no comas, no vivas* |
| **usted** | present subjunctive: *hable, coma, viva* | *no hable, no coma, no viva* |
| **nosotros** | subjunctive: *hablemos, comamos* | *no hablemos* |
| **vosotros** | infinitive − r + d: *hablad, comed* | *no habléis, no comáis* |
| **ustedes** | subjunctive: *hablen, coman* | *no hablen, no coman* |

**Irregular affirmative tú:** *decir → di*, *hacer → haz*, *ir → ve*, *poner → pon*, *salir → sal*, *ser → sé*, *tener → ten*, *venir → ven*.

**Pronouns** attach to the affirmative (*dímelo, siéntate, hazlo*) and go before the negative (*no me lo digas, no te sientes*).

## Use

- **Orders and instructions:** *Corta las patatas y fríelas.*
- **Advice:** *Descansa y bebe mucha agua.*
- **Requests:** *Pasa, por favor. Ven aquí.*
- **Softening:** add *por favor*, or use *¿Puedes…?* / *¿Podría…?* instead.

## Contrast with English

- English has one imperative (*Open the door*) for everybody; Spanish distinguishes **tú, usted, vosotros** and **ustedes**.
- English negatives use *don’t* (*don’t run*); Spanish negatives use the **subjunctive** (*no corras*).
- Spanish attaches pronouns to the verb (*siéntate*), where English adds a separate word (*sit down*).

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *No habla tan deprisa.* (for tú) → ✓ *No **hables** tan deprisa.*
- ✗ *Tienes que abrir la puerta* as an order → ✓ ***Abre** la puerta.*
- ✗ *Hacé el trabajo.* (for tú in Spain) → ✓ ***Haz** el trabajo.*
- ✗ *Ven en el parque.* → ✓ ***Ven al** parque.* / ***Ven a** mi casa.*

## How it sounds

Commands often come with falling intonation and clear stress: **AB**-re, **CO**-rre, **DI**-me. With a pronoun, the stress stays on the verb and the written accent shows it: **dí**-me-lo, **sién**-ta-te.
