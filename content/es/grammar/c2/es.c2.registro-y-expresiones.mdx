## In one sentence

**Register** is the level of formality of what you say or write, and **idioms and proverbs** are fixed expressions whose meaning is not literal: choosing them well is one of the clearest marks of a very advanced speaker.

## Form

| Register | Typical features | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Formal** | *usted*, subjunctive requests, nominal style, passives | *Se ruega que abonen la cuenta en caja.* |
| **Neutral** | *tú/usted* according to context, standard vocabulary | *Por favor, pagad en caja.* |
| **Informal** | short forms, interjections, slang, ellipsis | *Oye, paga en la caja, ¿vale?* |
| **Literary** | inversion, long periods, cultismos | *Cuán largo se hacía el camino.* |

**Idioms (modismos)** are fixed: *echar un vistazo* (to have a look), *estar en las nubes* (to be daydreaming), *dar en el clavo* (to hit the mark), *tomar el pelo* (to pull someone’s leg), *meter la pata* (to put one’s foot in it), *costar un ojo de la cara* (to cost a fortune).

**Proverbs (refranes):** *Más vale tarde que nunca. A buen entendedor, pocas palabras bastan. No hay mal que por bien no venga. Quien mucho abarca, poco aprieta.*

## Use

- **Match the register to the situation:** a complaint letter, an email to a friend and an academic essay need different words and different verb forms.
- **Use idioms sparingly:** one well-placed expression gives color; five in a paragraph sound forced.
- **Never translate an idiom word for word:** *costar un ojo de la cara* is not "to cost an eye of the face" for the listener who knows English; it is simply *to cost a lot*.
- **Know the cultural frame:** many proverbs come in pairs that say opposite things (*A quien madruga, Dios le ayuda* / *No por mucho madrugar amanece más temprano*).

## Contrast with English

- English often marks formality with vocabulary (*purchase* vs *buy*); Spanish adds grammar: *usted*, the subjunctive, *se* impersonal constructions.
- English idioms often use animals or body parts differently: *to let the cat out of the bag* is *irse de la lengua* or *descubrir el pastel* in Spanish.
- Many Spanish refranes are very short and rhythmic: *Ojos que no ven, corazón que no siente.*

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Tomar el cabello.* → ✓ ***Tomar el pelo.***
- ✗ *Poner la pata.* → ✓ ***Meter** la pata.*
- ✗ Using *tú* and slang in a formal letter → ✓ *usted* and a neutral or formal vocabulary.
- ✗ Piling up idioms in the same sentence → ✓ one per paragraph, and only when natural.

## How it sounds

Fixed expressions have their own rhythm, and speakers often say them faster than normal speech: *más-vale-tar-de-que-nun-ca*. In proverbs, the two halves are separated by a short pause: *Más vale tarde… que nunca.*
