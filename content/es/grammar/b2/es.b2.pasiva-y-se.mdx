## In one sentence

Spanish prefers **se** to the true passive in everyday speech: *se venden pisos*, *se habla español*, *se dice que…*; the passive with **ser + participle** is more formal and common in the news.

## Form

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **Passive se** (thing + verb in agreement) | *Se vende un piso. / Se venden dos pisos.* |
| **Impersonal se** (no object, or a person as object) | *Se vive bien aquí. / Se busca a los culpables.* |
| **ser + participle** (+ *por* + agent) | *La casa fue construida por mi abuelo.* |

- The participle agrees in gender and number with the subject: *construido, construida, construidos, construidas*.
- *Se* with a singular verb works for any general statement: *se dice, se sabe, se cree, se necesita*.

## Use

- **Passive se** is used for signs, instructions and general facts: *Se prohíbe fumar.* / *Se necesitan camareros.*
- **Impersonal se** talks about people in general (*one, you, they*): *Cuando se habla otro idioma, se descubren formas diferentes de ver el mundo.*
- **ser + participle** is typical of formal writing and history: *El edificio fue restaurado en 2010.* It is rarely used in conversation.
- In **estar + participle** the action is finished and we describe the result: *La tienda está cerrada.*

## Contrast with English

- English often uses *they*, *you* or *one* (*they say that…*); Spanish uses *se dice que…*.
- English passives are very common (*was built, is spoken*); Spanish prefers *se*, or an active sentence with an unspecified plural subject (*Dicen que…*).
- English *by* becomes Spanish *por*, but only with ser + participle.

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Se vende dos pisos.* → ✓ *Se **venden** dos pisos.* (the verb agrees with the thing sold)
- ✗ *Se busca a camareros.* → ✓ *Se **buscan** camareros.* (the "a" is only for specific people)
- ✗ *La puerta fue cerrada* to say it is closed now → ✓ *La puerta **está cerrada**.* (estar + participle describes the result)
- ✗ *La casa fue construido en 1900.* → ✓ *La casa fue **construida** en 1900.* (agreement of the participle)

## How it sounds

In **se** + verb, *se* is unstressed and joins the verb as if it were one word: *se venden* sounds like se-**VEN**-den. In the passive, the participle carries the stress: cons-tru-**Í**-da, vi-si-**TA**-da.
