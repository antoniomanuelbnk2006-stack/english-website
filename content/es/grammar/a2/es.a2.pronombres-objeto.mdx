## In one sentence

**Lo, la, los, las** are direct object pronouns: they replace the thing or person that receives the action, so you do not repeat it.

## Form

| | masculine | feminine |
|---|---|---|
| singular | **lo** (him, it) | **la** (her, it) |
| plural | **los** (them) | **las** (them) |

Other forms: *me, te, nos, os* work as direct and indirect objects: *Me ves. Te quiero. Nos llaman.*

- The pronoun goes **before the conjugated verb**: *La veo. No lo compro.*
- With an infinitive or a gerund it can go before the first verb or **attach to the end**: *Lo quiero comprar* = *Quiero comprarlo*; *La estoy viendo* = *Estoy viéndola*.

## Use

- **Avoid repeating:** *¿Tienes el billete? — Sí, lo tengo.*
- **With people:** *¿Ves a Ana? — Sí, la veo.*
- **In questions and answers:** *¿Dónde pones las llaves? — Las pongo aquí.*

## Contrast with English

- English puts the object *after* the verb (*I see it*); Spanish puts the pronoun **before** (*lo veo*).
- English uses *it* for every thing; Spanish chooses **lo** or **la** by gender: *el libro → lo*, *la casa → la*.
- With infinitives, English keeps the pronoun separate (*to buy it*); Spanish can attach it (*comprarlo*).

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Veo la.* → ✓ ***La** veo.*
- ✗ *Compro lo.* → ✓ ***Lo** compro.*
- ✗ *Quiero verlos las películas.* → ✓ *Quiero **verlas**.* (the pronoun agrees with *las películas*)
- ✗ *Lo veo a Ana.* → ✓ ***La** veo a Ana.* (a woman → la)

## How it sounds

The pronoun is weak and joins the verb: *lo compro* sounds like one word, **lo-COM-pro**. When attached (*comprarlo*), the stress stays on the verb: com-**PRAR**-lo.
