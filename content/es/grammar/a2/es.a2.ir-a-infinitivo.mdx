## In one sentence

Use **ir a + infinitive** for plans and things that are about to happen, and **tener que + infinitive** for what you must do.

## Form

| | ir a + infinitive | tener que + infinitive |
|---|---|---|
| yo | **voy a** comprar | **tengo que** comprar |
| tú | **vas a** comprar | **tienes que** comprar |
| él / ella | **va a** comprar | **tiene que** comprar |
| nosotros | **vamos a** comprar | **tenemos que** comprar |
| vosotros | **vais a** comprar | **tenéis que** comprar |
| ellos | **van a** comprar | **tienen que** comprar |

- The verb after *a* or *que* is **always the infinitive**: *voy a **comer***, not *voy a como*.
- Negative: *no* goes before *ir* or *tener*: *No voy a ir.* *No tengo que trabajar.*

## Use

- **Plans and intentions**: *Este verano voy a viajar a Italia.*
- **Near future**: *Cuidado, vas a caerte.*
- **Obligation**: *Tengo que llegar antes de las seis.*
- **Questions about plans**: *¿Qué vas a hacer mañana?* *¿Vais a venir a la fiesta?*

## Contrast with English

- *Ir a + infinitive* works like English *going to*: *I am going to buy* → *voy a comprar*. But Spanish needs the **a**: *voy comprar* is wrong.
- *Tener que* is the equivalent of *have to* (a real obligation). For a softer duty, Spanish also uses *deber*.
- Spanish often uses the **simple present** for plans as well: *Mañana viajo a Madrid.*

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Voy comprar un pastel.* → ✓ *Voy **a** comprar un pastel.*
- ✗ *Tengo estudiar.* → ✓ *Tengo **que** estudiar.*
- ✗ *Vamos a compramos.* → ✓ *Vamos a **comprar**.* (infinitive after *a*)
- ✗ *Ella tiene que a ir.* → ✓ *Ella tiene que **ir**.* (no *a* after *que*)

## How it sounds

In speech, *voy a* often sounds like one word, **voy-a**, and *tengo que* like **ten-gó-que**. The stress in *voy*, *va*, *vas*, *van* falls on the only syllable; in *vamos* it is **VA**-mos, and in *tenemos* it is te-**NE**-mos.
