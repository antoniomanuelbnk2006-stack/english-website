## In one sentence

In formal Spanish the mood after a connector shows **how the speaker sees the information**: as a known fact (indicative), as a hypothesis or an unimportant fact (subjunctive), or as an intention (subjunctive, with *para que*).

## Form

| Meaning | Connector | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| Concession, known fact | *aunque, a pesar de que, si bien* | indicative |
| Concession, hypothesis | *aunque, por más que, así* | subjunctive |
| Purpose, new subject | *para que, a fin de que* | subjunctive |
| Purpose, same subject | *para, a fin de* + infinitive | infinitive |
| Condition | *con tal de que, siempre que, a menos que, a no ser que* | subjunctive |

- *Por más que, por mucho que, por muy… que* are concessive and take the subjunctive for hypotheses and the indicative for known facts: *por mucho que insistas, no cambiará de opinión.*
- *Sin que* and *antes de que* always take the subjunctive: *se fue sin que nadie lo viera.*

## Use

- **Known fact:** *Aunque es difícil, merece la pena.* The speaker accepts that it is difficult.
- **Hypothesis:** *Aunque sea difícil, merece la pena.* The speaker does not accept it as a fact, or does not consider it important.
- **Purpose:** *Escribo para que me entiendan.* / *Lo repito para entenderlo mejor.* (same subject: infinitive).
- A relative clause with an unknown or non-existent antecedent also takes the subjunctive: *Busco un texto que explique el problema con claridad.*

## Contrast with English

- English *although* + the same verb forms for fact and hypothesis; Spanish marks the difference with the mood: *aunque llueve* (it is raining) versus *aunque llueva* (even if it rains).
- English *so that* + indicative-looking verb (*so that he can understand*) needs the subjunctive in Spanish: *para que pueda entender*.

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Aunque sea lloviendo, salimos.* → ✓ *Aunque **llueve**, salimos.* (a known fact)
- ✗ *Te lo explico para que entiendes.* → ✓ *Te lo explico para que **entiendas**.*
- ✗ *Voy a estudiar para que apruebe.* (same subject) → ✓ *Voy a estudiar **para aprobar**.*
- ✗ *A pesar de que sea tarde, cenamos.* (known fact) → ✓ *A pesar de que **es** tarde, cenamos.*

## How it sounds

The difference between *aunque llueve* and *aunque llueva* is a single vowel: the **-e** of the indicative and the **-a** of the subjunctive. Native speakers lean on a slightly rising intonation after *aunque* + subjunctive, because the hypothesis is still open.
