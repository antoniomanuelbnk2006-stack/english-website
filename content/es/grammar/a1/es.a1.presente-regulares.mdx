## In one sentence

Regular Spanish verbs end in **-ar**, **-er** or **-ir**: to use them in the present tense, drop that ending and add the ending that matches the person.

## Form

| | -ar (*hablar*) | -er (*comer*) | -ir (*vivir*) |
|---|---|---|---|
| yo | habl**o** | com**o** | viv**o** |
| tú | habl**as** | com**es** | viv**es** |
| él / ella / usted | habl**a** | com**e** | viv**e** |
| nosotros | habl**amos** | com**emos** | viv**imos** |
| vosotros | habl**áis** | com**éis** | viv**ís** |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | habl**an** | com**en** | viv**en** |

**-er** and **-ir** verbs share almost every ending (*-es, -e, -en*); they differ only in *nosotros* and *vosotros* (*-emos / -imos*, *-éis / -ís*).

## Use

- **Habits and routines**: *Trabajo por las mañanas.*
- **Facts and things that are true now**: *Vivo en Valencia.*
- **Near-future plans**, often with *mañana*: *Mañana estudio con Luis.*
- **Questions**: just change the intonation and add *¿ ?*: *¿Hablas español?*

## Contrast with English

- English changes the verb only in the third person (*he works*). Spanish changes it for **every** person, so the ending tells you who is speaking and the subject pronoun is usually dropped.
- English needs *do* in questions and negatives (*Do you work? I don't work*). Spanish does not: *¿Trabajas? No trabajo.*
- One Spanish form covers both English *I work* and *I am working*: *Trabajo* = both.

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Yo trabajar en una tienda.* → ✓ *Yo **trabajo** en una tienda.* (do not use the infinitive)
- ✗ *Ella habla**s** inglés.* → ✓ *Ella **habla** inglés.* (-s is only for tú)
- ✗ *¿Haces tú hablar inglés?* → ✓ *¿**Hablas** inglés?* (Spanish has no *do*)
- ✗ *No yo hablo francés.* → ✓ ***No** hablo francés.* (*no* goes right before the verb)

## How it sounds

The stress falls on the **stem** in *yo*, *tú*, *él*, *ellos*: **HA**-blo, **HA**-blas, **HA**-bla. In *nosotros* it moves to the ending: ha-**BLA**-mos. Every vowel is short and clean: *comemos* is co-**ME**-mos, never co-**MEE**-mos.
