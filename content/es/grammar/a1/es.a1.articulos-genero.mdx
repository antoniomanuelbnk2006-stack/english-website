## In one sentence

Every Spanish noun has a **gender** (masculine or feminine) and a **number** (singular or plural), and the article and adjectives around it must agree.

## Form

| | masculine | feminine |
|---|---|---|
| the (singular) | **el** libro | **la** casa |
| the (plural) | **los** libros | **las** casas |
| a / an | **un** libro | **una** casa |
| some | **unos** libros | **unas** casas |

- **Gender clues**: nouns ending in **-o** are usually masculine (*libro, perro*), and those ending in **-a** usually feminine (*casa, mesa*). Exceptions: *el día, la mano, el mapa*.
- **Plural**: add **-s** after a vowel (*casa → casas*) and **-es** after a consonant (*ciudad → ciudades*).
- **Adjectives agree too**: *un libro nuevo, una casa nueva, unos libros nuevos, unas casas nuevas*.

## Use

- Use **el / la / los / las** for something specific or already known: *La casa de Ana es grande.*
- Use **un / una / unos / unas** for something new or not specific: *Tengo una hermana.*
- People with a profession or nationality after **ser** usually take **no article**: *Soy profesora.* *Es español.*

## Contrast with English

- English has no grammatical gender for things: *the book*, *the house*. In Spanish every noun is masculine or feminine, and you have to learn the gender **with** the noun (*la mesa*, not just *mesa*).
- English has one *the* and one *a*. Spanish has four forms of each, and the adjective changes too.
- Spanish uses *el / la* in places where English uses none: *la vida es bonita* (life is beautiful), *me gusta el café* (I like coffee).

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *El casa es grande.* → ✓ ***La** casa es grande.*
- ✗ *Una libro nuevo.* → ✓ ***Un** libro nuevo.*
- ✗ *Los niños bonitas.* → ✓ *Los niños **bonitos**.* (the adjective agrees)
- ✗ *Soy una profesora.* → ✓ *Soy profesora.* (no article for a profession)

## How it sounds

**El** and **la** are short and unstressed: say them as part of the noun (*el-LI-bro*, *la-CA-sa*). The final **-s** of **los**, **las**, **unos** and **unas** is a light /s/. Don't pronounce **un** like English *"un"*: it is a short *oon* sound.
