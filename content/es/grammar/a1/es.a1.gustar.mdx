## In one sentence

With **gustar**, the thing you like is the **subject** of the sentence and you are the indirect object: *Me gusta el café* means "coffee is pleasing to me".

## Form

| Who likes it | One thing (singular / verb) | Several things |
|---|---|---|
| a mí | **me gusta** el café | **me gustan** los libros |
| a ti | **te gusta** | **te gustan** |
| a él / ella / usted | **le gusta** | **le gustan** |
| a nosotros | **nos gusta** | **nos gustan** |
| a vosotros | **os gusta** | **os gustan** |
| a ellos / ellas / ustedes | **les gusta** | **les gustan** |

- Negative: put **no** in front: *No me gusta el café.*
- With a verb: *Me gusta bailar.* (always **gusta**, even with two verbs: *Me gusta leer y escribir.*)
- Add **a + name or pronoun** for clarity or emphasis: *A Marta le gusta el té.* *A mí me gusta, pero a ti no.*

## Use

- **Likes and dislikes**: *Me gusta la música. No me gustan las arañas.*
- **Degrees**: *me gusta mucho / bastante / un poco.*
- **Asking**: *¿Te gusta…? ¿Qué te gusta?*

## Contrast with English

- English says *I like coffee*, with *I* as the subject. Spanish says *coffee pleases me*: the verb agrees with the thing liked, not with who likes it.
- Because of that, **gusta / gustan** depends on the **thing**: *me gusta el libro* (one), *me gustan los libros* (several).
- With people, *me gusta Ana* usually means you find her attractive. To say you like someone as a person, use **me cae bien**: *Ana me cae bien.*

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Yo gusto el café.* → ✓ ***Me gusta** el café.*
- ✗ *Me gusta los libros.* → ✓ *Me **gustan** los libros.* (several things → plural)
- ✗ *Me gustan bailar.* → ✓ *Me **gusta** bailar.* (a verb counts as one thing)
- ✗ *A mí gusta el té.* → ✓ *A mí **me** gusta el té.* (the pronoun is not optional)

## How it sounds

In **gusta** and **gustan**, the stress is on **GUS**: **GUS**-ta, **GUS**-tan. The *g* in *gusta* is a hard /g/ as in *go*, and the *u* is a short *oo* sound, never as in English *"gust"*.
