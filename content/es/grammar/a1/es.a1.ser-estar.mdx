## In one sentence

Spanish has two verbs for *to be*: **ser** says what someone or something *is* (who, what, where from, what like), and **estar** says where it *is* and how it is *right now*.

## Form

| | ser | estar |
|---|---|---|
| yo | **soy** | **estoy** |
| tú | **eres** | **estás** |
| él / ella / usted | **es** | **está** |
| nosotros | **somos** | **estamos** |
| vosotros | **sois** | **estáis** |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | **son** | **están** |

Both are irregular: learn them as whole tables, not by rule.

## Use

- **Ser**: identity and profession (*Soy Marta. Es médico.*), origin and nationality (*Somos de Chile.*), characteristics (*La casa es grande.*), time and dates (*Son las tres. Hoy es lunes.*).
- **Estar**: location (*Estoy en casa. Madrid está en España.*), temporary state and feelings (*Estás cansado. Estamos contentos.*), and *how are you?* (*¿Cómo estás?*).
- A quick test: if you can say "right now" without changing the meaning, it is probably **estar**.

## Contrast with English

- English has one verb, *to be*, for everything. Spanish makes you choose: *I am a teacher* → *soy profesora*, but *I am at home* → *estoy en casa*.
- Spanish usually drops the subject pronoun: *soy*, not *yo soy*, unless you need emphasis.
- The adjective can change meaning with the verb: *es aburrido* (he is boring) vs *está aburrido* (he is bored).

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Soy en casa.* → ✓ ***Estoy** en casa.* (place: estar)
- ✗ *Estoy profesora.* → ✓ ***Soy** profesora.* (profession: ser)
- ✗ *Yo soy cansado.* → ✓ *Estoy cansado.* (a state, not an identity)
- ✗ *Madrid es en España.* → ✓ *Madrid **está** en España.*

## How it sounds

In **está**, **estás**, **estáis** and **están** the stress falls on the written accent: es-**TÁ**. **Es** and **son** are one short syllable each. The *s* in **soy** and **somos** is a clean /s/, and **eres** is **EH-res**, with a soft single *r*.
